Revision impact on course-material satisfaction

Too early to measure — impact is pending, not negative.

Student feedback is quarter-level and currently spans only 2026 Q1, Q2 and a just-started Q3. A clean before/after read needs a full pre-revision and a full post-revision quarter. Because most revisions went live in Q2 or Q3 2026, their post-revision students have not been surveyed yet: across all 27 matched revised courses, Q3 currently holds only 11 responses. We are therefore not reporting per-course impact yet — the movements available today sit inside sampling noise (post-revision quarters at n≈5) and cannot be attributed to the revisions. When can we meaningfully measure each cohort?

A cohort becomes measurable once a full pre-revision quarter (already in the data) and a full post-revision quarter both exist. The post-revision quarter is the one after go-live (the go-live quarter itself mixes pre- and post-revision students), and its feedback lands when that quarter closes.

Reading this: by Q4 2026 we can meaningfully look at 9 courses (the Q2-2026 go-lives, once Q3 feedback closes). By Q1 2027 that grows to 25 cumulatively (adding the 16 Q3-2026 go-lives, once Q4 feedback closes). In short: anything revised during Q3 2026 is measurable by Q1 2027 — its baseline quarter (Q2 2026) is already captured and its first full post quarter (Q4 2026) closes in January. Revisions shipping in Q4 2026 or later slip to Q2 2027+.

Caveats: counts cover the 27 of 40 revised courses that appear in this feedback export; the other 13 have no feedback rows (too few responses or not offered these quarters) and may never reach a usable sample. Even when measurable, cohort samples are small (n often single digits) — treat results as directional. The 2 course(s) revised in Q1 2026 cannot be cleanly measured here because their baseline quarter (Q4 2025) predates the export.
